# Spike 0001: performance budget and plan (b18) on Edge (NVIDIA)

Date: 2026-11-26 (UTC). Machine: the owner's Linux laptop (RTX 3080 Laptop GPU). Code: branch `spike-0001/budget`, on
top of `spike-0001/small-batch` (#82). Every figure below is quoted from, or computed from, the reports in this
directory. Estimates and assumptions are labelled as such.

## Why

Since GEMM v3 (#71), the default review has stayed about 2× native KataGo. The work after it was one experiment at a
time, chosen from the largest slice of the latest profile, with no target or ceilings. This document sets the target,
measures what each kind of work costs today against what this device can do, and turns that into a plan of phases.
Each phase has a goal, a predicted saving and a stop rule.

## How it was measured

- **Budget benchmark** (new, the page's "Budget benchmark" button, `test/gpu/budget-bench.js`), in the separate test
  Edge with no extra switches:
  - It warms the GPU for 2 s.
  - It measures the device's ceilings: copy bandwidth, vector FMA throughput (f32 and f16), matrix throughput where
    available, and a near-empty dispatch.
  - At batches 1, 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64, it times the network's pass three ways: whole, without each kind of dispatch,
    and with only that kind. The three are timed next to each other, each repeated 3 times; the median is shown, and
    the reports also have the least and greatest.
  - Timing is passes submitted back to back after a warm-up (`timing.js`), from GPU timestamps.
  - The network is the search benchmark's: `{"winograd":true,"fuse":true,"precision":"f16"}` (GEMM v3
    `{rm:4, rn:8, tk:16}`), built for maxBatch 64.
  - Report: [`…-edge-nvidia.json`](2026-11-b18-budget-edge-nvidia.json) (2026-11-26T16:27:19Z), with its
    nvidia-smi clock log [`…-edge-nvidia-clocks.csv`](2026-11-b18-budget-edge-nvidia-clocks.csv).
- **Least work per dispatch** (new, `GpuNetwork.opWork`):
  - `flops` counts 2 per multiply-add. The Winograd transforms are counted as dense 6×6 matrix products.
  - `bytes` counts each input, weight and output element once, at its stored size (an accumulated output is read and
    written).
  - A kind's **floor** is the larger of flops ÷ the measured FMA throughput and bytes ÷ the measured copy bandwidth.
    The floor is a lower bound, not something a kernel can be expected to reach.
- **Native:** `katago benchmark` with the native review's config (`analysis_example.cfg`, plus
  `numSearchThreads` as `benchmark` requires).
  - Run as `-v 1600 -n 10 -t 4,8,16,32,64,128 -no-server-thread-test`.
  - It used one GPU thread with `useFP16 = true useNHWC = true`.
  - Log, with progress lines removed: [`…-native-benchmark.txt`](2026-11-b18-budget-native-benchmark.txt).
  - No clock log was recorded for it.
- **A first run of the budget benchmark is superseded:** [`…-edge-nvidia-run1.json`](2026-11-b18-budget-edge-nvidia-run1.json)
  (16:25:25Z). It had no global warm-up and timed each value once. Its first copy measurement (64 GB/s) ran while the
  clocks were still rising, and single timings gave negative shares and one 165.7 ms outlier. Those flaws were fixed
  before the run above.

**Power cap:** under this sustained load the GPU ran at its software power cap (throttle reason `0x4`) in 501 of 561
samples with utilisation ≥ 90%. Clocks were 480–1830 MHz, median 1500; power had a median of 107.34 W and a maximum of
110.21 W. A review keeps the GPU this busy for about 30 s (#81). All browser figures here are under that cap.

## Today against the floors, batch 16

Share = whole pass − pass without the kind; alone = pass with only the kind. Efficiency = floor ÷ time, for both.
Floors use 343.4 GB/s and 20.28 TFLOPS.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| Winograd GEMM | 78 | 12.4893 | 13.0417 | 3.69 | arithmetic | 28–30% |
| 1×1 convolution (GEMM) | 41 | 5.168 | 5.0275 | 1.54 | arithmetic | 30–31% |
| Winograd input transform | 78 | 3.3798 | 3.6513 | 1.70 | memory | 47–50% |
| Winograd output transform | 78 | 3.1832 | 2.9959 | 1.90 | memory | 60–63% |

| matvec | 11 | −0.2153 | 0.2715 | 0.00 | — | — |
| Global pooling | 7 | 0.5805 | 0.0468 | 0.01 | — | — |
| **Whole pass** | 338 | 26.2799 | | 9.61 | | 37% |

At batch 64 the GEMMs are at 29–35% of the FMA floor and the transforms at 51–67% of the copy floor. All batches are
in the report.

**What the floors say:**
- **GEMMs:** the two GEMM kinds take 17.5–18.2 ms of the 26.28 ms pass, at about 30% of this GPU's measured vector
  FMA throughput.
- **Transforms:** the Winograd transforms take 6.2–6.8 ms, at 47–63% of the measured copy bandwidth.
- **What parity needs:** the floors add up to 9.61 ms. Matching native (15.54 ms) needs the pass at 62% of its floors
  overall; today it is at 37%. A breakdown that would reach it: GEMMs at 60% of FMA throughput (8.72 ms), transforms
  and batch norm at 80% of copy bandwidth (5.44 ms), and matvec and pooling as today (0.32 ms alone), 14.48 ms in all.
- **Whether that's reachable:** nothing measured yet shows WGSL kernels reaching 60% or 80% here, so this is the
  hypothesis Phases 1 and 2 test.
- **Matrix hardware:** with it, the GEMMs' arithmetic floors drop below their memory floors. The Winograd GEMM is
  then bound by the V and M tensors it reads and writes: 1725.2 MB per pass at batch 16 across the input transform,
  GEMM and output transform, 5.02 ms at copy bandwidth. The floors add up to 8.32 ms with matrix hardware against
  9.61 without, a reduction of only 1.29 ms. Matrix hardware pays off much more only together with removing that
  traffic (fusing the transforms into the GEMM).

## The plan

One phase at a time. Each ends either finished (goal met) or closed (written up with what was done, what remains and
why it stopped). Every report gives the change from the prior result and the ratio to native: review `totalMs` (batch
16, two at once) against native's 16412.1–16596.1 ms, and batch-16 evaluations/s against native's 1029.36.

### Phase 1: GEMM efficiency (vector units, no browser switch)

- **Why first:** largest share (17.5–18.2 ms at batch 16), furthest below its floor (about 30%), and it's the path
  every user's browser has.
- **Goal:** both GEMM kinds at ≥ 60% of measured FMA throughput at batch 16.
- **Predicted saving if met:** GEMMs about 8.7 ms, saving about 9 ms of the 26.28 ms pass (about −35%). This is
  arithmetic from the floors, not a measurement.
- **Method, measurement first:** a ladder in the GEMM benchmark at the batch-16 Winograd shape (M = 192, N = 400,
  K = 192, 36 matrices) and the 1×1 shapes:
  1. The inner loop from workgroup memory only (no global loads), against FMA throughput.
  2. The global-to-workgroup loads only, against copy bandwidth.
  3. The whole kernel.

  The stage furthest from its own ceiling is changed first. Then the ladder runs again.
- **Checks for every change:** output identical to GEMM v2 and the CPU reference (GEMM benchmark); the check page's
  goldens; then the traced review and the native comparison.
- **Stop rule:** if, after fixing each limit the ladder shows, the GEMM is still below 45% of FMA throughput at batch
  16, close the phase.

### Phase 2: Winograd transforms (memory-bound)

- **Goal:** both transforms at ≥ 80% of copy bandwidth at batch 16.
- **Predicted saving if met:** 6.2–6.8 ms down to about 4.5 ms, about −2 ms (arithmetic from the floors).
- **Method:** the same ladder (reads only, writes only, the whole transform).
- **Stop rule:** below 70% after fixing what the ladder shows, close the phase.

### Phase 3: matrix hardware, your decision with numbers

- It is available only behind `--enable-unsafe-webgpu`.
- After Phases 1 and 2 I'll bring the measured numbers: the matrix GEMM's efficiency, and the floor with and without
  fusing the transforms into it.
- You decide whether to finish that path. I won't start it on my own.

### Phase 4: the rest

- Batch norm (48–69% of its floor).
- The review's time off the GPU (about 6%).
- The evaluation count (about 6%, an estimate). That's a search question, checked against native's behaviour.

Each gets a goal and stop rule from the figures at that point.
